Internal Audit Complete Pack
User Guide & File Index

A complete, ready-to-use set of internal audit documents prepared in accordance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017, Clause 8.8 — from annual planning and audit preparation, through on-site execution and nonconformity management, to corrective-action closure and report archiving. This guide explains the role of each file, the order in which to use them, and the essentials for a successful rollout.
Basis: ISO/IEC 17025:2017 §8.8 English Version v1.0 11 Files Full-Element Checklist §4.1–§8.9

1What This Pack Solves

Internal audit is a mandatory requirement of ISO/IEC 17025:2017, Clause 8.8 — and one of the most common sources of nonconformities raised by assessors. Typical assessor concerns include:

Common assessor challengeTypical laboratory gapCorresponding file in this pack
“How does your audit programme reflect a risk-based approach?”The annual plan is just a department rota — no risk analysis, no frequency rationaleLP-02 §5.1 + IA-01 (with risk-grading examples)
“Does your checklist cover every element?”The checklist merely paraphrases the standard; no verification methods, no evidence fieldsIA-04 Full-Element Checklist (§4.1–§8.9, clause by clause)
“Are auditors independent of the work being audited?”Small laboratories lack staff, so auditors audit their own workLP-02 §3, §5.2 (independence management + external auditor route)
“Is the root-cause analysis of nonconformities adequate?”Analysis stops at “human error”; corrective action equals re-trainingFR-24 (root-cause guidance) + IA-06 (verification and closure)
“Were last audit's findings actually fixed?”Nonconformity reports are filed and forgotten; no trend analysisIA-05 Distribution & Trend Analysis + IA-06 Tracking & Verification

3The Nine-Step Audit Cycle

1 · Annual planningQuality manager prepares IA-01 per LP-02 §5.1 with risk-based frequencies; top management approves
2 · PreparationTeam leader issues IA-02 at least 5 working days ahead; auditors study relevant documents
3 · Checklist buildingAuditors follow IA-WI-01 and customise the clauses in scope from IA-04
4 · Opening meetingTeam leader briefs objective, scope, criteria and schedule; attendance recorded on IA-03
5 · On-site auditDocuments, records, observations and interviews; evidence captured on IA-04
6 · Raising nonconformitiesFacts confirmed with the auditee, then FR-24 issued and graded
7 · Closing meetingFindings and conclusions communicated; correction deadlines set; recorded on IA-03
8 · Report & analysisTeam leader issues FR-25 within 10 working days; IA-05 trend analysis completed
9 · Correction & closureResponsible departments correct within deadline (FR-24); auditors verify closure via IA-06; results feed management review

Established laboratory (annual rolling)

Prepare the next year's IA-01 every December; rotate by element block each quarter (management §4/§5/§8 → resources §6 → processes §7); audit high-risk areas (equipment, reporting, data management) every six months; complete year-end closure and consolidate IA-05 before November as management review input (§8.9.2 e)).

Relationship to the website template library: LP-02, FR-24 and FR-25 in this pack share the same codes and origin as their counterparts in the website's 81-file library and can be substituted or merged directly into your document list. IA-01 through IA-06 and IA-WI-01 are pack-exclusive supplements with independent numbering that do not conflict with the website library. All files can be adapted to your laboratory's name, department structure and scope of products — after modification, bring them under document control per your document-control procedure.
Usage notes: ① Replace all “[bracketed]” placeholders with your laboratory's actual information before formal issue; ② for checklist IA-04, print or enable only the clause pages in scope for each audit, to avoid a one-size-fits-all sheet; ③ the timeframes in this pack (5 working days' notice, 10 working days for the report, 15 working days for correction) are recommended values — you may adjust them in LP-02 when the procedure is controlled, provided the adjusted values are then applied consistently.
