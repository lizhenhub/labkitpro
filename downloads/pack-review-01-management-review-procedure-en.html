Management Review Procedure

Requires top management to systematically review the management system at planned intervals and to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness (§8.9.1), covering the full flow of the 15 inputs (§8.9.2 a)–o)) and the 4 outputs (§8.9.3 a)–d)).
Doc No. LP-09 Version V1.0 Corresponds to §8.9.1 / §8.9.2 / §8.9.3 Chaired by: top management (laboratory director)

1　Purpose

To standardise the laboratory's management review activities, ensure that top management systematically reviews the management system at planned intervals and evaluates its continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness in alignment with the quality policy and objectives (ISO/IEC 17025:2017 §8.9.1). The management review is the laboratory's top-level decision mechanism to “review the past and plan the future”: by consolidating one year of system-operation evidence, it identifies improvement opportunities and resource gaps and forms binding decisions for the next cycle.

2　Scope

This procedure applies to the management review of all departments, all testing/calibration capabilities and all sites covered by the management system (including fixed, temporary, mobile and external facilities), covering both the annual routine review and additional reviews triggered by major changes. It interfaces with the Internal Audit Procedure (LP-02) — internal audit conclusions are a key input to the management review (§8.9.2 e)).

4　Definitions

TermMeaning
Management reviewA systematic review by top management at planned intervals to ensure continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the system, in alignment with the policy and objectives (§8.9.1).
SuitabilityWhether the system still matches the laboratory's current reality (does it still “fit” after changes in internal/external factors, workload and scope).
AdequacyWhether the system elements are complete and cover all criteria and business requirements, without gaps.
EffectivenessWhether the system actually achieves the quality policy and objectives and ensures result validity (evidenced, not merely “done”).
Inputs / outputsInputs = the evidence and information reviewed (§8.9.2, 15 items); outputs = the decisions and measures formed (§8.9.3, 4 items).

5　Procedure

5.1　Review Planning (interval and triggers)

① The routine review is held at least once every 12 months (typically after internal audits are complete and before the next year's plan starts). The Quality Manager prepares the next year's Management Review Plan (MR-01) in Q4, specifying time, scope and input allocation, approved by top management.

② Top management may decide on an additional review when any of the following occurs: major scope extension/reduction; organisational or legal-entity change; major nonconformity in external assessments (accreditation body, regulator, customer); major complaint or result-validity incident; major change in resources (personnel/premises/equipment).

③ Once approved, the annual plan is loaded into the Management Review Input Preparation Calendar (MR-07), which issues milestone reminders to all departments.

5.2　Input Preparation (§8.9.2 a)–o), 15 items)

Key requirement: every input must carry “conclusion + evidence reference”; writing only “normal” is not acceptable. For example, item b must not say “objectives basically met” — it should say “5 of 6 annual objectives met; 1 unmet (report on-time rate 92%, target 95%), reasons in the body”.

5.3　Review Notice and Meeting Organisation

① At least 10 working days before the review, the Quality Manager issues the Management Review Notice & Implementation Plan (MR-02) with the agenda, input list and material submission deadline.

② Inputs are pre-reviewed before the meeting; major issues requiring decisions are identified and compiled into an “issues to be decided” list attached to MR-02.

③ The meeting is chaired by top management, minuted by the Quality Manager, producing the Management Review Minutes (MR-04; corresponds to website library FR-26/FR-43). Attendees sign the attendance page; key topics must reach an explicit “decided / not decided / postponed” conclusion.

5.4　Review Execution (four review dimensions)

The meeting proceeds on the following logic, judging each input in turn:

Review dimensionQuestions the chair should ask
SuitabilityDoes the system still fit? After changes in internal/external factors, scope and workload, do the policy, objectives and documents still match?
AdequacyAre any elements missing? Are new businesses / new regulations covered by the system? Are resources sufficient?
EffectivenessWere the objectives met? Is result validity evidenced? Were last year's decisions implemented? Are improvements effective?
ConsistencyAre the review conclusions consistent with the quality policy, accreditation requirements and customer expectations?

5.5　Outputs (§8.9.3 a)–d), 4 items)

After the review, the Quality Manager drafts the Management Review Report (MR-05) from the minutes. The outputs must record decisions and measures related to:

Item§8.9.3 outputWhat the report should reflect
aEffectiveness of the management system and its processesConclusion on overall system effectiveness (including suitability/adequacy/effectiveness judgement)
bImprovement of laboratory activities related to meeting this standardSpecific improvements (methods / reports / QC / internal audit, etc.) and responsibilities
cProvision of resources neededPersonnel, premises, equipment, training, funds to be added
dChanges neededDecisions on system documents, responsibilities, scope, policy and objectives
Output rule: every output must be an “executable decision + owner + completion deadline”; expressions such as “strengthen management” that cannot be verified are prohibited. All decisions are entered into MR-06 for tracking.

5.6　Decision Tracking and Closure

① The Quality Manager transfers all decisions in MR-05 to the Management Review Decision Tracker (MR-06) with owner, deadline and verification method.

② Decision completion is verified as input d) at the next review; overdue items without a reasonable explanation are escalated to top management.

③ System document changes arising from the review are revised, approved, issued and withdrawn per the Document Control Procedure.

5.7　Records and Archiving

The review generates the following records, retained for no less than one accreditation cycle (typically 6 years, or per customer/regulatory requirements): MR-01 plan, MR-02 notice, MR-03 inputs, MR-04 minutes, MR-05 report, MR-06 tracker, MR-07 calendar, and the original evidence for the inputs. The Quality Manager archives them as evidence for external assessments (§8.9.2 g)) and for system effectiveness.

5.10　Common Traps in Chaining and Deciding

① Becoming a briefing session: reading each department's material without any decision. Fix: prepare the “issues to be decided” list (MR-02 §4) and decide only on those. ② Decisions that cannot be verified: outputs written as “continuous improvement” or “strengthen management”. Fix: each output must have the four elements “decision + owner + deadline + acceptance criteria” (see MR-05). ③ Inputs without data: an item written only as “normal”. Fix: pre-review 5 working days before, return for correction, never hold the meeting with blank items. ④ Decisions not tracked: nobody follows up; next year's item d still says “in continuous progress”. Fix: transfer all to MR-06 within 3 days of issuing MR-05; check monthly; verify at closure. ⑤ Missing records: no attendance, no minutes. Fix: MR-04 must have an attendance page and item-by-item decisions, retained as the “management review records” required by the standard.

5.11　Triggers and Timing of Additional Reviews

When a major change described in §5.1 occurs (major scope extension/reduction, organisational or legal-entity change, major nonconformity in external assessment, major complaint or result-validity incident, major resource change), top management shall decide and organise an additional review within 1 month of the change, regardless of the 12-month interval. The additional review may narrow its scope (assessing only affected elements), but its outputs must still be entered into MR-06 and serve as input d) of the next routine review. An additional review does not replace the annual routine review, and the records of the two are retained with separate retention periods.

5.12　Internal Drivers and Value of the Management Review

Many laboratories treat the management review as a formalistic meeting to “satisfy accreditation”. This seriously underestimates its value. Its true value: it is the only mechanism where the laboratory's top level systematically “reviews the past and plans the future”, concentrating the operational evidence scattered across departments into one room where top management decides resources and changes on the spot, preventing problems from idling between departmental walls.

To make it work, the Quality Manager typically faces three kinds of resistance. The first is “no time to meet”: compress the meeting to half a day, decide only, and have informational material pre-circulated for self-study. The second is “don't know what to review”: strictly collect the 15 inputs item by item with MR-03 — with data on every item, the meeting naturally has content. The third is “no use holding it”: the root cause is usually untracked decisions; connect all outputs to MR-06, check monthly and verify at closure, so the review genuinely changes resource allocation and the document system.

From the assessor's perspective, the management review is a mandatory inspection focus. Assessors typically chase three lines: ① do the inputs cover all 15 items with evidence; ② do the outputs contain verifiable decisions with owners; ③ are decisions genuinely closed, confirmed in next year's inputs. The MR-03 / MR-05 / MR-06 trio in this pack is designed for exactly these three lines; following it significantly reduces the probability of observations.

5.13　Legal and Accreditation Effect of Review Records

Management review records are not only an internal improvement tool — they are key evidence for externally demonstrating system effectiveness. In accreditation assessments, supervisory inspections, customer second-party audits and dispute handling, assessors will review the full set of management review records to judge whether top management genuinely discharges system-maintenance duties. Completeness, authenticity and traceability of the records are therefore critical.

The evidentiary power rests on three pillars: process traces (from plan, inputs, meeting to report and tracking, each step signed and checkable); evidence traceability (every conclusion in the report maps to specific data and references in the inputs); verifiable closure (decisions form a complete chain from issue to closure, confirmed in the following year). Missing any link sharply weakens the probative value.

The Quality Manager should treat the records as the laboratory's “annual health report”: accumulate operational data conscientiously, present it truthfully at the review, and track decisions resolutely afterwards. Then the review turns from a burden into an asset — passing external assessments while genuinely driving continuous improvement.

5.14　Linking the Review to the Annual Improvement Plan

The outputs should not end in a report that is archived and forgotten. The correct approach is to break the four outputs into executable annual tasks tracked by the Quality Manager day to day: resource decisions become procurement or hiring plans; change decisions become document revision tasks; improvement decisions become targeted projects. Only then does the review drive the system to spiral upward and benefit the credibility of every testing datum.

Top management's involvement is indispensable in this link. The Quality Manager can organise, but resource investment and change decisions must be made and continuously followed by top management. It is recommended to make decision implementation a fixed item in the quarterly meeting, not a once-a-year review. With higher frequency, problems are found and corrected at an early stage instead of erupting at year-end.

The review should also be scheduled in coordination with the internal audit plan: complete the audit first, then use its conclusions as review input, forming the closed rhythm of “internal audit finds problems → review decides resources → daily tracking delivers”. Too long an interval makes inputs stale; too short means audit corrections have not yet shown effect. A general recommendation: start the management review within one month after the internal audit — fresh evidence, plus a window to observe correction results. With coordinated scheduling, the whole system forms a self-improving long-term mechanism.
