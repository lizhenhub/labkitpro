UKAS · Specific Requirements Beyond ISO/IEC 17025:2017

UKAS · Specific Requirements Relative to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — Explained

UKAS (United Kingdom Accreditation Service) is the UK national accreditation body (ISO/IEC 17011). Its defining feature: a UKAS-accredited laboratory must simultaneously comply with three layers of documents — UKAS-issued documents (GEN/LAB/TPS/M3003) plus the mandatorily adopted European EA policies plus the mandatorily adopted Global ACI (formerly ILAC) policies. ISO/IEC 17025:2017 is the criterion baseline; every additional requirement comes from this "three-layer stack".

!Key Points

In UKAS's "three-layer stack", the core combination laboratories must implement first is: traceability & uncertainty (TPS 41, M3003 / LAB 12, LAB 5), proficiency testing & report statements (TPS 47, TPS 53), assessment principles (GEN 1), plus the mandatorily adopted EA-4/02 and TECH-1-008/009, ILAC P10; the rest are on-demand or sector-specific documents selected by business need.

1

Relationship to ISO/IEC 17025:2017

Three-layer stack · mandatory adoption

UKAS uses ISO/IEC 17025:2017 (testing/calibration) and ISO 15189:2022 (medical; ISO 15195 for reference measurement laboratories) as its criteria. On top of the criteria, UKAS-issued documents govern "how it is done in the UK", EA policies govern "how European mutual recognition is done", and Global ACI policies govern "how international mutual recognition is done" — all three are mandatory, none can be dropped.

Layered document system

Layer 1 · ISO criterion baselineISO/IEC 17025:2017, ISO 15189:2022, ISO 15195:2018 (reference measurement laboratories)
Layer 2 · UKAS-issued (GEN/LAB/TPS/M3003)Assessment principles GEN 1/4/6, calibration reports LAB 5, uncertainty LAB 12/M3003, scopes LAB 45, decision rules LAB 48, traceability TPS 41, PT TPS 47, reference materials TPS 57
Layer 3 · Mandatorily adopted EA (Europe)EA-4/02 calibration uncertainty, EA-3/01 symbols & MLA references, EA-2/15 flexible scopes, EA-4/17 medical scope descriptions
Layer 4 · Mandatorily adopted Global ACI (formerly ILAC)TECH-1-008 (PT, replacing ILAC P9), TECH-1-009 (calibration uncertainty, replacing P14), ILAC P10 (traceability, to be replaced by TECH-1-011)
Layer 5 · Reference library (G/INF series)EA guidance and reference publications, Global ACI guidance (TECH-1-001~006, etc.) — for reference, not mandatory

Practical Points & Common Pitfalls

4 tips

The maintenance burden for UKAS users lies in the "three-layer stack"; the most commonly missed distinction is between the mandatory-adoption layer and the reference layer.

1Track all three layers at once

UKAS-issued (GEN/LAB/TPS) + the EA library + the Global ACI library — all three lists must be followed for revisions; watching only UKAS-issued documents will cause you to miss mandatory files such as EA-4/02, EA-3/01 and TECH-1-008/009.This is the most typical omission for UKAS users

2Distinguish mandatory (M) from reference (G/INF)

Documents marked M (mandatory) — EA-4/02, EA-3/01, EA-2/15, EA-4/17, plus TECH-1-008/009 and ILAC P10 — must be adopted; the G/INF series (EA-4/21, EA-4/23, TECH-1-001~006) is reference material.Treating reference as mandatory, or vice versa, will get your assessment baseline wrong

3Numbers are migrating — switch to the new number before citing

ILAC P9→TECH-1-008, P14→TECH-1-009, P10→TECH-1-011 (drafting); G7/G8/G17/G19/G26→TECH-1-001/002/003/004/006 — file documents under the new numbers.Prevents version loss caused by mixing old and new numbers

4Dense 2025–2026 revisions — verify the Edition before citing

LAB 12/13, LAB 60/61, TPS 71, IMA 1, EA-3/01, EA-4/21/23 have all been revised or newly issued; TPS 47 (Ed.6) and LAB 24 (Ed.3) are under review.Preparing an assessment against an old Edition is the most frequent error during revision periods
