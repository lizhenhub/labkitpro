Management Review Work Instruction

Step-by-step operating guidance for the management review: where to get the data for each of the 15 inputs, how to write a proper conclusion, how to chair the meeting, how to complete MR-05, how to run MR-06, plus a filling example bank, a pre-issue self-check, and time-saving tips.
Doc No. MR-WI-01 Version V1.0 Supports LP-09 Audience: Quality Manager / reviewers / department leads

1　Before You Start

This instruction is the “operating manual” for LP-09. Before acting, read LP-09 to understand the overall flow, then prepare this year's dates from MR-01/MR-07. Principle: the review's value equals the quality of its inputs and the tracking of its outputs — empty inputs and abandoned decisions are what turn a review into paperwork.

3　How to Write a Conclusion for Each Input (with examples)

Conclusion formula: “current value/state (data) → comparison (vs. target or vs. last year) → judgement (met / gap / risk) → suggestion (for decision)”. It must never end with “normal”.

Good: “PT satisfaction 100% in 8 rounds, internal QC in-control rate 98%, intermediate check completion 100%; result validity confirmed (item n).”

Poor: “No problems found in this item.” (data missing, no evidence, cannot support a judgement)

4　How to Chair the Management Review

① Start: state the purpose and scope; confirm inputs are complete; if any item is missing or “empty”, stop and return it. ② For each issue to be decided, the chair asks: “What is the evidence? What is the decision? Who owns it? By when?” — and the minutes record the answers. ③ Guard against drift into a briefing session: informational content is read beforehand, the meeting decides only. ④ Before closing, the chair reads back the four output categories a)–d) and confirms each has an owner and deadline. ⑤ Appoint the Quality Manager to enter everything into MR-06 within 3 days.

5　How to Complete MR-05 (step by step)

① Copy the 15 core conclusions from MR-03 into §3 (edit for one consistent voice). ② In §4 write the triple-dimension judgement: suitability / adequacy / effectiveness, each with evidence. ③ In §5 turn the “issues to be decided” decisions into the four output categories, each row must have: decision content + owner + deadline + acceptance criteria. ④ In §6 draft next year's objective proposals based on item b and changes. ⑤ Get the sign-off of the Technical Manager, then top management; issue within 10 working days after the meeting.

6　How to Operate MR-06 (decision tracker)

① Within 3 days of issuing MR-05, enter each output row by row (decision no., source category, content, owner, deadline, status). ② The Quality Manager checks monthly: remind owners 5 days before deadlines; overdue items without a reasonable explanation are escalated to top management in writing. ③ At closure: verification method + completion evidence + verifier signature; not-passed items are returned (record attempt no.). ④ Next year's input d) simply reads off the tracker's annual summary — the closure chain becomes the evidence.

8　Filling Example Bank

Two full “good vs. poor” pairs are provided as calibration references when departments fill in MR-03 — compare before submitting:

Poor pair (typical of a formalistic review):
Item e: “Internal audit passed with no major problems.”
Item l: “Resources adequate.”
Item d: “Previous decisions basically implemented.”
Review output: “Continuous improvement.” — no data, no evidence, no owner, no deadline; this whole review will not survive an assessor's review.

Good pair (the target of this pack):
Item e: “FR-25 No. 2026-02: 5 NCs, all minor, concentrated in §6.4 equipment records; conclusion — the system conforms and is effective.”
Item l: “Key equipment average utilisation 85%; GC queues over 3 days in peak months; 1 analyst position vacant; add 1 GC + 1 analyst (decision c).”
Item d: “11 of 12 decisions closed, 1 delayed with reasons recorded; closure evidence in MR-06.”
Review output: “c: Technical Manager to complete GC procurement, installation and traceability by 2027-03-31 (acceptance: equipment register + traceability certificates).”

10　Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Must the review be held once a year? At least once every 12 months; additionally whenever a major change occurs, then an additional review within 1 month may be narrower in scope (see LP-09 §5.11).

Q2: Who must attend? Top management, Quality Manager, Technical Manager, heads of departments and authorised signatory representatives; anyone who owns an issue must attend its decision.

Q3: Can the meeting be shortened? Yes: pre-circulate informational inputs, and decide only the issues on the “issues to be decided” list; half a day is enough for a typical laboratory.

Q4: What if a department's input is late? Return it with a deadline (5 working days before the review is the hard gate); the calendar (MR-07) and monthly reminders prevent this.

Q5: Is the review linked to internal audit? Yes: audit conclusions are input e); it is recommended to hold the review within one month after the audit so evidence is fresh and corrections have had time to show effect.

11　Quick Mnemonic (for the review day)

“3-3-3”: 3 musts — inputs with data, outputs with owners, decisions with deadlines; 3 checks — suitability, adequacy, effectiveness; 3 closures — report issued within 10 working days, decisions entered within 3 days, closure verified next year. Post this on the wall of the review room.

12　Time Traps (avoid these)

① Holding the review before the internal audit is complete — input e has nothing to cite; ② collecting inputs the day before the meeting — pre-review becomes a formality; ③ issuing the report months later — the decisions age before they start; ④ skipping monthly checks on MR-06 — next year's input d) becomes “all in progress”; ⑤ reviewing only for the accreditation body — the review degenerates into paperwork and loses its management value.
