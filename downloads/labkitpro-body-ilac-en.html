ILAC · Specific Requirements Relative to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — Explained

ILAC (International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation) is the global laboratory accreditation mutual recognition organisation (ILAC MRA). It is not itself an accreditation body: laboratories never apply to ILAC directly. Its policies take effect through the laboratory's national Accreditation Body (AB), which makes them binding on laboratories. Since 2026-01-01 ILAC has ceased operations and its functions are being consolidated into Global ACI (global-aci.org); document numbers are progressively transitioning to the TECH series.

!Key Points

Of the 29 files distilled across five areas, the ones laboratories must act on first are ILAC P10 (metrological traceability), ILAC P9 (proficiency testing) and ILAC P8 / R7 (symbols & claims); the rest are supporting or sector-specific and should be selected by business need.

1

Relationship to ISO/IEC 17025:2017

MRA-layer policy · enforced via national AB

ILAC does not itself issue testing/calibration criteria: international standards such as ISO/IEC 17025:2017 (testing/calibration) and ISO 15189:2022 (medical) are transposed and implemented by member accreditation bodies (ABs). The P/G/R series documents at the ILAC layer are "additional mutual recognition requirements" addressed to ABs and, ultimately, to laboratories — they become binding on laboratories once circulated by their national AB.

Layered document system

Layer 1 · International standard baselineISO/IEC 17025:2017, ISO 15189:2022, etc., transposed by the national AB (identical adoption)
Layer 2 · ILAC MRA-layer policies (P/R series)Traceability P10, proficiency testing P9, calibration uncertainty P14, symbols & claims P8/R7 — additional hard requirements once circulated by the national AB
Layer 3 · Implementation guidance (G series)General guidance on uncertainty, decision rules, scopes of accreditation, recalibration intervals + sector guidance for forensics/medical/inspection/horseracing
Layer 4 · Peer-evaluation assurance (A series)IAF/ILAC A1/A2/A3 ensure consistent AB implementation and verifiable MRA eligibility
Layer 5 · Global ACI succession (from 2026)ILAC functions consolidated into Global ACI; P9/P14/P10 etc. migrating to TECH-series numbers, old and new running in parallel during the transition

Practical Points & Common Pitfalls

5 tips

For a laboratory, the core of the ILAC layer is really only three things: traceability (P10), proficiency testing (P9), and symbols & claims (P8/R7). Everything else is supporting or sector-specific.

1Check the version before putting the MRA mark on export reports

A report/certificate claiming ILAC MRA accreditation must comply with P8 + R7; during the transition, first confirm the current number circulated by your national AB (it may already be the TECH series).Using an outdated symbol/claim format is the most common rework at the MRA layer

2The application channel is always your national AB

ILAC does not accept direct applications; your laboratory certificate's international MRA eligibility depends on your national AB passing the A2 peer evaluation.Don't look for an application portal at the ILAC layer

3Numbers are transitioning — old and new run in parallel

P9→TECH-1-008, P14→TECH-1-009, P10→TECH-1-011 (drafting); either number may appear during the transition — follow the version circulated by your national AB.Avoid locating migrated documents under the old number

4Sector documents apply on demand — don't treat them as general requirements

Forensics (G19), medical (G26), inspection (P15/G27/G28), horseracing/anti-doping (G7/G29) are all sector-specific — if unrelated to your business, they do not apply.Avoid misreading sector guidance as a hard requirement for everyone

5The archive site ≠ the current library

ilac.org is for historical reference only; document activity and current versions are on global-aci.org — verify the issue date before citing.Historical documents such as the R3 strategic plan still sit on the archive site and are easily mistaken for current ones
