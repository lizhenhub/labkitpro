NATA · Specific Requirements Relative to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — Explained

NATA (National Association of Testing Authorities, Australia) is Australia's national accreditation body. Its core mechanism: SAD 17025 (the ISO/IEC 17025:2017 Standard Application Document) transposes the international standard into NATA application requirements, then sector-specific SAC annexes (calibration / life sciences / animal health / infrastructure & asset integrity / legal-forensic / manufactured goods / materials) layer on Australia-specific requirements — "SAD application documents + SAC sector annexes + GAC general criteria" are the complete form of NATA's requirements beyond 17025.

1

Relationship to ISO/IEC 17025:2017

SAD application documents · SAC sector annexes

NATA does not rewrite 17025 clause by clause. Instead it issues SADs (Standard Application Documents) stating Australia's additional application requirements, then publishes sector SAC annexes to refine implementation, while GAC general criteria govern cross-cutting topics such as multi-site, symbol use, equipment and traceability.

Layered document system

Layer 1 · International baselinesISO/IEC 17025:2017 (testing/calibration), ISO 15189:2022 (medical), ISO/IEC 17020:2026 (inspection), 17043/17034, ISO 20387 (biobanking)
Layer 2 · Body rulesNATA Rules (revised 2026-09-11) + NATA Procedures for Accreditation (2026-08-25) — the master framework for accreditation management
Layer 3 · Standard Application Documents (SAD)SAD 17025 (core testing/calibration application document) + SAD 15189 / SAD 17020 / SAD GLP / SAD 17043 / SAD 17034
Layer 4 · Sector annexes (SAC) & general criteria (GAC)Calibration / life sciences / animal health / IAI infrastructure / legal-forensic / manufactured goods / materials annexes + general criteria on multi-site, symbols, equipment, traceability
Layer 5 · Guidance / scope / worksheets (GAG/SAG/SoA)Technical guidance, Scope of Accreditation activity/service descriptors (SoA), assessment worksheets (the application-assessment interface)

Practical Points & Common Pitfalls

5 tips

The NATA system runs on three tracks — "SAD + SAC + specialist schemes"; what is most easily missed is the sector annex and the withdrawn list.

1SAD 17025 is the core application document

SAD 17025 is NATA's application document for 17025 (2024-10-15) — assessments check against the SAD plus the sector SAC annex, not just the ISO text.The SAD is where NATA's additional requirements actually live

2Select your sector by the SAC annex

Calibration / life sciences / animal health / IAI infrastructure / legal-forensic / manufactured goods / materials each have "an annex general section + sub-sector annexes" — lock the sector group first, then pick the sub-sector.Pick the wrong annex = the wrong assessment baseline

3Match the specialist scheme to your business type

Medical follows SAD 15189 (effective 2026-02), inspection follows the 17020:2026 SAD (effective 2026-10); GLP/PTP/RMP/biobanking/imaging/SDS/respiratory are separate tracks.Body type differs, so the SAD and the underlying standard differ

4Apply with the worksheet + SoA

Self-assess clause by clause with the 17025 Assessment Worksheet, file your scope per the matching SoA descriptors; scope changes go through the Application for Scope Changes.Scope-table format follows the SoA wording

5Dense 2026 revisions + withdrawn list — check before citing

Rules/Procedures, SAD 15189, 17020:2026 and the manufactured-goods series are all revised in 2026; the website's "superseded" section is continuously updated — check it before citing any technical document.Arriving at assessment with withdrawn guidance is the most common maintenance-period error
