Internal Audit Work Instruction

Takes the Internal Audit Procedure (LP-02) down to the operating layer: how to build a checklist, how to sample, how to interview and gather evidence, how to grade and write nonconformities — with a built-in case library. This is the training text for first-time auditors.
Doc No. IA-WI-01 Version V1.0 Supporting LP-02 Operating layer: build / sample / interview / grade

1　Scope

This instruction is for use by audit team leaders, auditors, audited departments and quality personnel, in conjunction with LP-02 and the full set of audit forms (IA-01 through IA-06, FR-24, FR-25). Where this instruction conflicts with the procedure, LP-02 prevails; this document details how to execute, and does not change any responsibilities or flow.

2　How to Build an Audit Checklist

Follow these steps to turn the full-element checklist (IA-04) into the working checklist for this audit:

StepOperation
1. Select clauses in scopeCopy the clause pages of IA-04 that fall within this audit's scope (per IA-02); do not carry all pages every time.
2. Tailor the audit itemsAdjust the audit items to this audit's actual focus: e.g. for an equipment-focused audit, keep every §6.4 item and add items on first-use records after calibration and confirmation of calibration results.
3. Pre-set sampling pointsFill in, before the audit, the sampling plan per IA-02 §4 (which months, which records, which on-site actions), so the checklist guides the day rather than being filled out in hindsight.
4. Add “evidence to collect”In the evidence column, note the specific document/record numbers you will seek — a checklist that records evidence at the moment of finding it is reliable; one completed afterwards is not.

3　How to Sample (the core audit skill)

Sampling rules of thumb that keep findings defensible:

RuleApplication
Cover spread, not just the first pageSample evenly across the period in scope (by month), not only the most recent or most convenient files.
Minimum three per itemAt least 3 records per audit item; for high-risk items (report sign-off, data integrity) take 5–8.
Trace one end to endFor process items, follow one sample/item from start to finish — e.g. one customer request → sampling → analysis → report → archive — rather than looking at steps in isolation.
Include the exceptionalDeliberately sample the unusual: records after equipment repair, first batch signed by a newly authorised person, night-shift records, corrective-action cases.
State the sampleRecord exactly what was sampled (dates, numbers, counts) so the auditee can reproduce the finding.

4　How to Interview and Gather Evidence

TechniqueApplication
Ask about real cases“Show me the last report you signed” is evidence; “do you know the procedure?” is opinion. Prefer asking for demonstrations over hypothetical answers.
Three confirmation channelsAn audit statement is confirmed when document + record + observation or interview agree. A finding from only one channel is usually an observation, not a nonconformity.
Listen, then verifyWhen staff describe a process, ask them to open the actual record and walk you through it — the walkthrough is the evidence.
Record facts, not peopleCapture dates, document numbers, quantities and locations; avoid naming individuals in the finding. Nonconformities address the system, not the person.
Confirm facts on the spotAt the closing meeting, or immediately after the finding, read the fact statement back to the auditee and obtain confirmation (FR-24 §1).

5　How to Grade and Write Nonconformities

GradeCriteria
MajorAbsence or complete loss of a requirement; a nonconformity that directly undermines result validity; a finding that recurs after a previous correction.
MinorA single occurrence, partial nonconformity, no direct impact on result validity — e.g. one record missing one field.
ObservationA potential weakness or improvement opportunity, no current nonconformity demonstrated.

Poor writing: “Report quality is poor” — a judgement, not a fact; cannot be verified or corrected.

Good writing: “Report No. TR-2026-031 (issued 2026-02-18) does not state the sampling location required by §7.8.3; the sampling record shows the site was A001. The template revision TR-2025-02 in use also omits this field.” — objective, citable, fixable.

6　Case Library (typical findings and how to phrase them)

AreaTypical findingHow to phrase it
Equipment (§6.4)Instrument used before the calibration result was confirmed after return from calibration“Instrument SN-2210 was used for report TR-2026-020 on 2026-02-10; the calibration certificate Cal-2026-014 was only confirmed by the Technical Manager on 2026-02-15. The equipment management procedure LP-09 §6.4.13 requires confirmation before use.”
Reporting (§7.8)Sampling information missing from a report“Report TR-2026-031 does not state the sampling location; the sampling record shows site A001. §7.8.3 requires the report to include all sampling information.”
Records (§7.5)Observation written in pencil / electronic record without audit trail“The pH record for sample S-0218 on 2026-01-12 is written in pencil; §7.5.1 requires amendments to be legible and traceable.”
Traceability (§6.5)Intermediate check overdue, plan without criteria“The monthly intermediate check of the analytical balance has no recorded criteria or tolerance; §6.4.6 requires checks to have defined criteria.”
Complaints (§7.9)Complaint registered but no response record“Complaint CM-2026-004 (received 2026-03-02) has no record of response or closure decision within the 5-working-day requirement of §7.9.2.”

7　What to Do After the Audit (report, correction, closure)

StageOperation
Report (FR-25)Team leader drafts within 10 working days; every finding cites IA-04 evidence; effectiveness judgement supported by IA-05 trend data.
Corrective actions (FR-24 / §8.7)Immediate correction, then root-cause analysis (person/method/equipment/material/environment), then root-cause-targeted action; owner + deadline; update the risk register.
Verification (IA-06)Verify effectiveness with sample re-checks and non-recurrence over the following period; close or return per the escalation rules in IA-06 §3.
Feed forward (IA-05 → management review)Consolidate distribution and trend; submit as management review input e) and as evidence for next year's audit programme (IA-01).
Common traps: ① copying the standard into the checklist instead of turning it into actions; ② auditing only documents, never on-site work; ③ grading every finding as minor to avoid conflict; ④ writing findings about people instead of systems; ⑤ accepting “we'll improve” as a correction instead of verified evidence of change.
