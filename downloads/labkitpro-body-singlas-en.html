SINGLAS · Specific Requirements Relative to ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — Explained

SINGLAS (the laboratory accreditation scheme of the Singapore Accreditation Council) is Singapore's national laboratory accreditation system, managed by SAC under the Enterprise Singapore framework. Mechanism: SAC-SINGLAS 001 Accreditation Process (issued separately for the 17025 and 15189 editions) + sector-specific requirements (MET/CE/EL/MECH/NDT/C&B-ENV/IT/FFT/MED) + four Technical Guides on measurement uncertainty + LAFM assessment checklists. In the medical field, documents are selected by MED 002 sub-discipline (8 in total).

!Key Points

Of the 41 documents distilled across the system, the ones a SINGLAS laboratory must put in place first are the 7 core files: SAC 01 (Terms and Conditions), SAC 02 (Rules for Use of Accreditation Marks and MRA Marks), SAC-SINGLAS 001 (Accreditation Process), SAC-SINGLAS 004 (Classification of Tests), SAC-SINGLAS 006 (Traceability of Measurement), PROF 001 (Policies on Proficiency Testing) and LAFM 03 (the 17025 assessment checklist); sector series, Technical Guides and the MED series are then selected by your field of business.

1

Relationship to ISO/IEC 17025:2017

Accreditation Process + sector-specific requirements

SINGLAS implements ISO/IEC 17025:2017 through SAC-SINGLAS 001 (Accreditation Process). Testing/calibration laboratories additionally apply MET/CE/EL/MECH/NDT/C&B-ENV/IT/FFT sector-specific requirements, while medical laboratories follow the separate 15189 edition process and the MED series — sector documents are the core vehicle of SINGLAS's additional requirements.

Layered document system

Layer 1 · International baselineISO/IEC 17025:2017 (testing/calibration), ISO 15189:2022 (medical); supported by the ISO-ILAC-IAF joint communiqué on 17025 management systems
Layer 2 · SAC general rulesSAC 01 Terms and Conditions, SAC 02 Rules for Use of SAC Accreditation Marks and MRA Marks, SAC-SINGLAS 004 Classification of Tests, PROF 001 Policies on Proficiency Testing
Layer 3 · Accreditation processSAC-SINGLAS 001 Accreditation Process (ISO/IEC 17025:2017 edition 2026-03-09; ISO 15189:2022 edition 2024-09-06)
Layer 4 · Sector-specific requirementsMET/CE/EL/MECH/NDT/C&B-ENV/IT/FFT series + medical MED 001 General Criteria and the 8 MED 002 sub-discipline documents
Layer 5 · Technical guidance & checklistsTechnical Guide 1–4 (uncertainty guides), LAFM 03 / 03-MED assessment checklists, Guidance Note series

Practical Points & Common Pitfalls

5 tips

The SINGLAS system is clearly divided by sector series; the most common pitfalls are selecting the wrong medical sub-discipline document and failing to check versions.

1For medicine, pick MED 002 by sub-discipline

Clinical chemistry / cytogenetics / cytopathology / haematology / histopathology / genetics & genomics / microbiology / molecular pathology — 8 sub-disciplines each have their own MED 002; choose the one matching your business.Picking the wrong sub-discipline means using the wrong document set

2Select sector-specific requirements by series

Calibration MET, chemical/biological/environmental C&B-ENV, civil CE, electrical EL, mechanical MECH, NDT NDT, IT security IT, functional foods FFT — lock the series first, then read the documents.Every addition beyond the 17025 general clauses lives in the sector series

3Four uncertainty guides plus sector specials

TG1 general (2026-03-18) / TG2 chemical & microbiological / TG3 civil & mechanical / TG4 medical (2025-11-03), plus the sector specials EL 002 and Guidance Note NDT 001.Match the guide to your laboratory's business

4Use the LAFM 03 checklists by edition

LAFM 03 (17025 edition, updated 2026-03-27) and LAFM 03-MED (15189 edition) are separate — self-check against the correct one before assessment.Medical labs using the wrong checklist get sent back

5Dense 2025–2026 updates — verify the version first

SAC 01/02 (2026-01), IT 001 (2026-08), the MED series (2025), C&B-ENV (2026-03), TG1 (2026-03), PROF 001 (2026-04) have all been revised — check the date before citing.Bringing outdated documents to an assessment is the most common error during a revision wave
