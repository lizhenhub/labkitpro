Conclusions & advice
  1. The control plan is the OUTPUT of the PFMEA (AIAG APQP/PPAP and IATF 16949 clause 8.5.1.1): every process step should map to a PFMEA failure mode and be backed by detection/control here.
  2. All special characteristics have a reaction plan — consistent with AIAG expectations.
  3. Prefer error-proofing (poka-yoke) and SPC for critical/significant characteristics over final inspection alone.
  4. The report includes the full control-plan table and summary — export to Word for filing or customer/audit submission.

About this tool

A Control Plan is the living document that specifies how each product and process characteristic is controlled - what is measured, with what gage, how often, and what to do when it goes wrong. It is the bridge between a PFMEA (which ranks the risks) and the shop floor (which executes the control), and it is a required IATF 16949 deliverable.

This tool gives you an eight-column control-plan table you can extend row by row: process step, product characteristic, process characteristic, special-characteristic classification (Significant / Critical), measurement technique, sample size and frequency, control method and reaction plan. Pre-filled with a machined-part example so you can adapt it to your own line in minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a special characteristic?

A special characteristic is one whose variation can significantly affect safety, compliance or customer fit, so it gets extra control and is flagged (Significant or Critical) on the drawing and the PFMEA. The Control Plan must carry the same classification and a tighter reaction plan than ordinary characteristics.

How is the Control Plan linked to the PFMEA?

Every high-risk PFMEA failure mode should map to a control in the Control Plan: the process characteristic you monitor comes from the PFMEA cause, and the reaction plan echoes the PFMEA recommended action. Keeping the two in sync is a standard IATF 16949 audit check - a PFMEA risk with no corresponding control is a finding.

Does the Control Plan change after SOP?

Yes. The Control Plan is revised as the process matures - lessons from launch, new PFMEA findings, or a process change trigger an update, and a pre-launch and production version are normal. Treat it as a living document, not a one-time deliverable.
