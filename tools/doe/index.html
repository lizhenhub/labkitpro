About this tool

Design of Experiments (DoE) lets you study several factors at once and separate the ones that actually matter from the noise. A 2^k full factorial sets every factor at two levels (low/high) across all combinations, so you get clean, independent estimates of each main effect and every two-factor interaction from the minimum number of runs.

After the runs, the analysis runs ANOVA to test which effects are significant, then plots the significant effects so you can see direction and size at a glance. Use it to optimise a process, screen method parameters, or qualify a measurement system - the same engine backs the screening studies behind a good method validation.

Frequently asked questions

How many runs does a 2^k factorial need?

For k factors the full factorial needs 2^k runs at the corner points, plus optional centre points to estimate curvature and pure error. Two factors = 4 runs, three factors = 8 runs. This tool builds the Yates order automatically, so you just enter the response for each run.

How do I tell a significant effect from noise?

Run ANOVA: an effect is significant when its F-ratio against the error term gives a p-value below your threshold (commonly 0.05). Without replicate runs, this tool uses the highest-order interaction as the error term for k >= 3; with centre-point replicates it uses the pure-error estimate. Always confirm significance against a stated error term, never by eye.

When should I add centre points?

Add centre points whenever you suspect the true response is curved (a factor has a sweet spot, not a straight trend). They give a curvature test and a pure-error estimate for the ANOVA - without them a 2-level design cannot detect curvature and has no independent error term for k < 3.
