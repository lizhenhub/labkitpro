Conclusions & recommendations
  1. Level 3 is the default PPAP submission level unless the customer specifies otherwise.
  2. The Part Submission Warrant (PSW) must be signed by an authorized representative before submission.
  3. Ensure MSA studies and initial process studies (Cpk/Ppk) are current and based on the approved control plan.
  4. Elements 2, 3, 4, 13, 15, 16 are level / customer dependent — confirm against the customer’s specific requirements.
  5. Retain the master sample and qualified-laboratory documentation for the customer’s required retention period.

About this tool

The Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) is how a supplier proves to the customer that the production process can reproduce the approved part at the quoted rate and quality, before a full run ships. It is built from 18 possible elements - design records, authorized engineering change, FMEAs, control plan, MSA, dimensional results and more - packaged to a submission level the customer specifies.

This tool gives you the full 18-element checklist with the required submission level marked for Levels 1-5 (Level 3, the default, requires a PSW with most supporting evidence submitted), plus a Part Submission Warrant (PSW) form and a live completion tracker. PPAP is the Phase 4 evidence gate of APQP and the companion to the Control Plan.

Frequently asked questions

What are the PPAP submission levels?

Levels 1-5 scale how much evidence the supplier submits to the customer (versus retaining it). Level 1 = PSW only; Level 2 = PSW + samples + limited data; Level 3 = PSW + samples + full supporting data (the most common default); Level 4 = PSW + customer-defined evidence; Level 5 = PSW + records at supplier location. The customer mandates the level.

What is the PSW?

The Part Submission Warrant (PSW) is the one-page sign-off that formally declares the part meets all requirements and states which submission level and results apply. It is the cover document of every PPAP package and must be authorized by a responsible supplier representative.

When is a PPAP required?

A new part or part revision, a changed process, tooling transfer, a supplier change, or any disruption that could affect fit/form/function all trigger a PPAP. The customer defines the exact triggers and level in the supply agreement; treat the 18-element list as the master checklist for each.
