Conclusions & recommendations
  1. This worksheet is built on the AIAG APQP (2nd ed.) · IATF 16949 phase framework — it plans and tracks new-product quality-planning deliverables and milestones, not a statistical or metrological decision.
  2. Overall completion is 30%; the earliest incomplete phase is "Phase 1 — Plan & Define Program" (75%). Prioritize closing its deliverables.
  3. Phases should advance in sequence: an earlier phase’s outputs (e.g. DFMEA, PFMEA, control plans) are inputs to the next. Complete MSA and initial process-capability studies before the production trial run.
  4. The report includes per-phase deliverable completion, the timing plan and improvement advice — export to Word for filing or review.

About this tool

Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) is the structured, cross-functional method the automotive industry uses to develop a product and its process so that customer requirements are met on the first try. It organizes the work into five phases - Plan and Define, Product Design, Process Design, Validation and Feedback - each with defined outputs that gate the next phase.

This tool turns the APQP framework into a trackable worksheet: select a phase, work through its deliverables, watch completion build up per phase and across the program, and lay out the timing milestones. It is the planning backbone that feeds Control Plan and PPAP, the other two IATF 16949 core tools.

Frequently asked questions

What are the five APQP phases?

Phase 1 Plan and Define (voice of customer, program charter); Phase 2 Product Design and Development (design FMEA, design verification); Phase 3 Process Design and Development (process flow, PFMEA, Control Plan); Phase 4 Product and Process Validation (trial run, PPAP, MSA, SPC); Phase 5 Feedback, Assessment and Corrective Action (launch, lessons learned). Each phase has outputs that gate the next.

Is APQP only for automotive?

It originates in the automotive IATF 16949 regime (AIAG), but the phase-gate logic applies to any new product or process introduction where getting it right first time matters - medical devices, aerospace and electronics teams adopt the same structure. The tool keeps the AIAG phase definitions and works for any disciplined NPD program.

How does APQP connect to PPAP and Control Plan?

APQP is the umbrella plan; the Control Plan and the PPAP submission are two of its key Phase 3-4 outputs. The Control Plan defines how each characteristic is monitored, and PPAP packages the evidence that the process can reproduce the approved product. Build all three together to avoid rework at launch.
