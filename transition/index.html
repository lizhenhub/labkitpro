02Shared vs standalone list (what changes once, what stays per-standard)

  • Shared, done once: gap analysis against the two new editions, the context and interested-parties register (incl. the climate call), policy wording, planning of changes, and the four procedures — document control, internal audit, corrective action, management review.
  • Standalone: environmental aspects and compliance obligations (14001), product and service controls (9001), worker participation and OH&S risk (45001), and operational controls per discipline — the soul of each standard; integration must not dilute them.
  • One register, three tags: every risk / opportunity / nonconformity is tagged quality / environment / safety — a defective valve on a chemical line can be all three. Share the workflow, keep discipline-specific criteria (45001 risk = likelihood × severity of harm; 14001 works with aspects and impacts), and never force one scoring scale onto all three.
  • Accountability across disciplines: risk-acceptance criteria are set by your own rules and funnel into one management review — one risk committee for three disciplines, not three parallel review boards.

Note: the chapter map follows the Annex SL high-level structure and ISO/BSI integration guidance; ISO 45001:2027 is still at DIS stage, so its new-edition changes are provided on an “expected” basis and the pack updates free of charge once published.

The audit-time account: the integrated-audit cap is 20%How much does integration actually save? The accreditation answer: the reduction is capped at 20% of the combined audit time. Larger numbers quoted in marketing have no source. This document covers the rule, what can and cannot be saved, and how to talk to your certification body.▾
TP-IMS-02Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01The rule

  • 20% is the hard cap — Under the accreditation framework (Global ACI — ILAC and IAF combined operations from 1 January 2026), a certification body may audit an integrated system in one combined visit, but the reduction is capped at 20% of the sum of the standalone audit times. A bigger “discount” does not exist within accreditation — whoever promises more is either off-script or cutting your audit coverage.
  • What is saved is structural duplication, not audit depth — The saving comes from three places: three document libraries become one, three audit programmes become one, three reviews become one — the audited object itself shrinks. What cannot be saved is the audit depth of each discipline’s operational controls: aspects, product controls and OH&S risks are checked as before.
  • Put the transition audit inside the next surveillance — Transition can happen at a scheduled surveillance or recertification audit, or as a separate visit. For three-certificate holders: change the shared elements once and negotiate one combined transition audit — the 14001 (2029-04-30) and 9001 (2029-09-30) cutoffs are close enough to plan together.
  • Audit days are the certification body’s own calculation — The rule sets the cap; actual days are each certification body’s own calculation. Two bodies may quote differently for the same system — normal, but get it in writing: which audit carries each transition, how many days, and at what cost.

Data red line: this pack does not quote marketing figures like “integrated certification saves 20-35%” or “register maintenance time down 40-60%” — they came from unsourced claims and were retracted by their own publisher. Only the verifiable 20% cap and the structural-saving rationale are used.

The six-phase roadmap (quality first, environment layered, OH&S closed)Integration is not blending three files at once — the proven path runs six phases: 9001 has the strongest process framework, so it is the backbone; 14001 and 45001 layer on top; documents, audits and the certification audit merge in turn. Adaptable to organizations of any size.▾
TP-IMS-03Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01The six phases

  • Phase 1: 9001 as the foundation — Quality management offers the strongest process framework. Use the existing QMS as the backbone and layer environmental and safety requirements onto it — not a third system from scratch. Action: run the quality-side gap analysis against the two 2026 editions and set governance (a single management representative).
  • Phase 2: add 14001 — Identify environmental aspects, compliance obligations and operational controls, mapped into the existing clause structure. Action: expand the context analysis (climate / biodiversity / pollution / resources), land the new 6.1.4/6.3 documents, upgrade the compliance register — shared parts (context register, change planning) done once for both.
  • Phase 3: layer in 45001 — Bring in hazard identification, risk assessment, worker consultation and the hierarchy of controls. ISO 45001:2018 remains the certifiable edition — this phase pre-wires the interfaces: a safety column in the shared register, a safety row in the competence matrix, with the standard-side revision after the new edition publishes.
  • Phase 4: unify documentation — Merge three sets of policies, manuals and procedures into one document family. Action: one numbering scheme, one integrated manual (built on this pack’s skeleton), the shared-document map fully tagged, and the four procedures (document control / audit / CAPA / review) merged.
  • Phase 5: integrated internal audits — Train cross-discipline internal auditors and build a combined schedule covering every clause of all three. Action: one integrated internal audit over all clauses (discipline criteria kept separate), one report with conclusions per standard, and nonconformities in a single CAPA stream.
  • Phase 6: combined certification audit — Choose a certification body accredited for all three standards and run one integrated certification audit. Action: lock the arrangement in writing per the CB checklist; the integrated-audit time reduction caps at 20% (see its dedicated document); the 45001 transition follows as a third wave after the new edition publishes.

The three-step method (ISO official: Prepare → Connect → Integrate)Following ISO’s official Integrated management systems practical guide, break the integrated transition into three steps and nine actions — this is your project execution framework.▾
TP-IMS-04Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01The three steps

  • Step 1 · Prepare — Leadership alignment and scope: top management commits to one transition for all three; define the integrated scope (sites / products / activities); appoint a single management representative for the integrated system; estimate the share of common documents. Key move: get the three transition timelines (14001 earliest, 2029-04-30) and let the earliest cutoff set the project cadence.
  • Step 2 · Connect — Map each standard’s requirements onto existing processes: use the shared-chapter map to mark shared / shareable / standalone; map the two new editions’ changes (9001 culture & split, 14001 expansion & change management) into shared processes; build a shared risk-opportunity register (quality / environment / safety in columns). Key move: by the end of this step you hold a three-way requirements→process→document matrix.
  • Step 3 · Integrate — Close gaps and embed into daily operations: revise shared documents per the matrix; combine the audit programme and run one management review; embed culture, change and emergency routines into existing meetings and dashboards rather than parallel mechanisms. Key move: the acceptance test is “an auditor can’t tell this was three systems stitched together” — one numbering scheme, one record format, one dashboard.

02Three-step self-check (9 actions)

  • Prepare: top management signs one integrated-transition decision (one document, not three).
  • Prepare: integrated scope and site list finalized; a single management representative appointed.
  • Prepare: three cutoffs on the wall, with 14001 (2029-04-30) setting the pace.
  • Connect: mark the shared-chapter map item by item (shared / shareable / standalone).
  • Connect: map the new-edition changes into shared processes (9001 culture & split; 14001 expansion, change management, emergency scope).
  • Connect: shared risk-opportunity register built (quality / environment / safety columns, one mechanism).
  • Integrate: shared documents revised; one numbering scheme and format.
  • Integrate: run one combined audit programme cycle; one management review covering all three.
  • Integrate: culture, change and emergency routines embedded in existing meetings and dashboards.

Five common obstacles and how to beat themAnnex SL makes integration possible; organizational reality makes it hard — the five most common failure points, each with a workable countermeasure. Read before you start; cheaper than rework later.▾
TP-IMS-05Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01The five obstacles

  • Obstacle 1: split departmental ownership — Quality, environment and safety usually sit in different teams with different reporting lines, so nobody owns the whole after integration. Countermeasure: clear governance — appoint a single management representative (or IMS manager) for all three; the discipline leads stay as domain owners; cross-cutting topics go to one risk committee.
  • Obstacle 2: maturity gaps — A QMS may have run for a decade while the EMS launched last year — aligning uneven maturities creates fake sharing. Countermeasure: run an honest maturity assessment first, and integrate from the most mature system (why Phase 1 of the roadmap is 9001-first); newer systems close their basics before joining the shared frame.
  • Obstacle 3: staff resistance — Staff used to their own discipline see merged processes as extra burden. Countermeasure, three parts: training (how the shared flow works), visible leadership commitment (the management representative explains integration wins in routine meetings), and embedding shared records into existing forms (one form with three tags, not three forms).
  • Obstacle 4: auditors competent in all three are scarce — Combined audits need audit teams competent across quality, environment and OH&S — not every certification body can staff them. Countermeasure: make “accredited for all three + able to field a cross-discipline team” a precondition in the CB checklist; build your internal-audit bench the same way.
  • Obstacle 5: integration diluting discipline depth — Shared documents written to the lowest common denominator sand off each standard’s specific requirements — the number-one integration failure and an auditor favourite. Countermeasure: write the manual by the three-lens rule (every shared clause reads through quality / environment / safety), flag standard-specific additions explicitly, and keep discipline criteria (scoring, evaluation scales) as standalone versions.

The integrated-manual skeleton (chapter by chapter)What does “one manual for three standards” actually look like? Chapters 4-10, each with the shared wording and the per-standard flags — build the skeleton from this and save yourself a blank-page week.▾
TP-IMS-06Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01Chapter-by-chapter skeleton

Controlled headerID / version / applicable standards

Controlled ID and version as usual; list the applicable standards line by line (ISO 9001:2026, ISO 14001:2026, ISO 45001:2018 + Amd1:2024) — a three-line “meets all requirements of the following standards” footer per page, with a slot ready to swap when ISO 45001:2027 publishes.

Chapter 4Context: one analysis, three lenses

One shared context and interested-parties register: internal/external issues and stakeholder expectations with quality / environment / safety dimension columns; the climate-relevance decision recorded explicitly (not-applicable only when argued); 45001’s interested parties must include workers.

Chapter 5Leadership: one policy meeting three 5.2s

Each standard keeps its own 5.2 policy subclause — one integrated policy with quality / environment / safety sections, each meeting its 5.2 content requirements. 45001’s clause 5 is “Leadership and worker participation” with its own 5.4 on consultation: the manual needs a worker-consultation section and channel records.

Chapter 6Planning: one register, three tags

One shared risk-and-opportunity register, every entry tagged quality / environment / safety; discipline criteria stay separate (45001 risk = likelihood × severity of harm, 14001 works with aspects and impacts) — share the workflow, not the scoring. Note: 9001:2026 and 14001:2026 both carry 6.3 planning of changes while 45001:2018 has no 6.3 — write the change procedure as “merged + an OH&S-impact branch”.

Chapter 7Support: one competence matrix, one communication plan

Share resources, competence, awareness, communication and documented information: one competence matrix (rows = roles, columns = quality / environment / safety requirements), one communication plan (internal/external channels plus worker consultation), and one documented-information control on the “availability” basis for all three. Discipline-specific competence needs stay in their own columns.

Chapter 8Operation: per-standard controls, unified templates

Operational control is the standalone zone: product/service control (9001), aspect control and emergency (14001), hazard hierarchy and emergency (45001) each written separately — but on unified process-control templates with one intake for nonconforming outputs. The two emergency-preparedness clauses (14001 8.2 / 45001 8.2) can share one chapter with two sections.

Chapter 9Performance evaluation: one integrated audit, one review

Share one annual integrated audit programme (criteria listed per discipline), one management review (inputs/outputs matched in three blocks), and one monitoring dashboard (complaints, energy/emissions, injury rates on one screen). Audit objectives are defined per audit as the 2026 editions require — integrated audits included.

Chapter 10Improvement: one CAPA stream for all three

Share the nonconformity and corrective-action workflow: one stream, three tags, root-cause analysis reused across disciplines (one trip can be a quality defect, an environmental event and a near-miss at once). 14001:2026 has no 10.1 — cite 10.2/10.3; 45001’s incident investigation is written as the front end of 10.2.

02Manual acceptance test (four “cans”)

  • Every shared clause reads through three lenses: flip to any page and a quality / environment / safety reader each finds their requirement — if not, you wrote a single-system manual.
  • Standard-specific requirements are explicitly flagged: life-cycle perspective, worker consultation, hierarchy of controls — marked in the manual so an auditor working to any single standard can find them.
  • Clause references trace to all three standards: procedure citations support the three-line annotation (or a mapping table), so old- and new-edition references stay traceable through the transition window.
  • No visible stitching: one numbering scheme, one manual, one CAPA stream, one dashboard — “an auditor can’t tell this was three systems” is the acceptance line.

The certification-body checklist (8 items to confirm in writing)The last piece of the transition puzzle sits between you and your certification body — audit days are their own calculation and combined audits depend on what they offer. This checklist gets all eight key questions answered, in writing, with costs locked.▾
TP-IMS-08Applies to：Three-System Integrated Transition Pack (9001 + 14001 + 45001)Rev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01Eight written confirmations

  • Accreditation: the body holds valid accreditation for ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 and can field a cross-discipline audit team.
  • Integration offer: does the body provide integrated audits? If so, how is the schedule built, how is the team composed, and how are results reported (conclusions per standard, or one report with sections)?
  • Transition carrier: which audit carries each transition — the next surveillance, the recertification, or a separate transition visit — with time and cost differences for each.
  • Days and pricing: the body calculates its own days — require it in writing: total days for the integrated audit, how the 20% cap is applied, and an itemized quote.
  • Combined transition: can the 14001 (cutoff 2029-04-30) and 9001 (cutoff 2029-09-30) transitions run as one combined audit (shared elements changed once)? A separate audit is the body’s call.
  • The 45001 handling: confirm ISO 45001:2018 + Amd1:2024 stays the certifiable edition, with a third transition wave scheduled once the new edition publishes under Global ACI’s own timetable.
  • Evidence compatibility: how the body accepts dual clause references during the transition window — can records carry both old- and new-edition clause citations?
  • Calendar lock: after confirming the three transition-audit dates plus the integrated audit and review dates, put them on the certification calendar — confirmed in writing, never by word of mouth.

  • List the shared controlled documents (use the map): one numbering scheme, one revision, effective for all three. Done when: the shared share is quantified (aim ≥60%) and the directory is merged.
