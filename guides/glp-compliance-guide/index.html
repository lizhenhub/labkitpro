Good Laboratory Practice (GLP): OECD Principles vs ISO/IEC 17025

Two different frameworks for two different questions — don't confuse them.

What GLP actually covers

Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) is a quality system for the conduct of non-clinical safety studies — the OECD Principles of GLP (and the regional adoptions, e.g. Australia's NICNAS/APVMA alignment, NZ's EPA adoption). It governs how studies are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, and reported so that data supporting regulatory submissions (pharma, agrochemical, cosmetics) are reliable. GLP is about study integrity, not the competence of a routine test method.

GLP vs ISO/IEC 17025:2017

DimensionGLPISO/IEC 17025:2017
ScopeNon-clinical safety studiesTesting & calibration competence
Question answered"Is the study data trustworthy?""Is the lab competent to test/calibrate?"
Core artefactStudy plan + raw data + final reportManagement system + uncertainty budgets
RegionOECD + AU/NZ adoptionsGlobal (ILAC mutual recognition)

Which does your lab need?

  • Routine testing / calibration for customers → ISO/IEC 17025:2017 accreditation (the document set LabKitPro generates).
  • Non-clinical safety studies for regulators → GLP compliance (study-plan and archive discipline).
  • Both → many contract research labs hold 17025 for method competence and run GLP for the studies themselves.
