<h1 style="font-size:27px;margin:14px 0 6px;">Good Laboratory Practice (GLP): OECD Principles vs ISO/IEC 17025</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;">Two different frameworks for two different questions — don't confuse them.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">What GLP actually covers</h2>
<p><strong>Good Laboratory Practice (GLP)</strong> is a quality system for the <em>conduct</em> of non-clinical safety studies — the OECD Principles of GLP (and the regional adoptions, e.g. Australia's NICNAS/APVMA alignment, NZ's EPA adoption). It governs how studies are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, and reported so that data supporting regulatory submissions (pharma, agrochemical, cosmetics) are reliable. GLP is about <em>study integrity</em>, not the competence of a routine test method.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">GLP vs ISO/IEC 17025:2017</h2>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">Which does your lab need?</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Routine testing / calibration for customers</strong> → ISO/IEC 17025:2017 accreditation (the document set LabKitPro generates).</li>
  <li><strong>Non-clinical safety studies for regulators</strong> → GLP compliance (study-plan and archive discipline).</li>
  <li><strong>Both</strong> → many contract research labs hold 17025 for method competence and run GLP for the studies themselves.</li>
</ul>
