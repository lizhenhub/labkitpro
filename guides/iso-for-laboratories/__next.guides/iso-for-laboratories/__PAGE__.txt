<h1 style="font-size:27px;margin:14px 0 6px;">ISO for Laboratories: Which Standard Do You Actually Need?</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;">For most testing and calibration labs the short answer is <strong>ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong> &mdash; but here is the nuance so you pick right the first time.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">The three standards you will hear about</h2>
<p>Search "ISO for laboratories" and you will keep running into three acronyms. They are not competitors &mdash; they cover different things.</p>
<table style="width:100%;border-collapse:collapse;font-size:14px;margin:10px 0;">
  <tr style="background:#0f2a4a;color:#fff;"><th style="padding:8px;text-align:left;">Standard</th><th style="padding:8px;text-align:left;">What it covers</th><th style="padding:8px;text-align:left;">Who needs it</th></tr>
  <tr><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;"><strong>ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong></td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">Technical competence for testing &amp; calibration labs</td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">Labs issuing test/calibration results to customers</td></tr>
  <tr style="background:#fafcfe;"><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;"><strong>ISO 9001</strong></td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">General quality management system</td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">Any organization wanting a QMS certificate</td></tr>
  <tr><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;"><strong>GLP</strong> (Good Laboratory Practice)</td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">Conduct of non-clinical safety studies</td><td style="padding:8px;border-bottom:1px solid #e6e9ef;">Pharma, agrochemical, environmental study labs</td></tr>
</table>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">Which one is yours?</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Testing or calibration lab serving external customers</strong> &rarr; you almost certainly need <strong>ISO/IEC 17025</strong>. It is the only ISO standard built for your technical competence, and it is recognized through the ILAC network in 100+ economies.</li>
  <li><strong>You only need a general QMS certificate</strong> &rarr; <strong>ISO 9001</strong> may be enough &mdash; but note it does <em>not</em> cover technical competence, so laboratories are usually asked for 17025 on top.</li>
  <li><strong>Non-clinical safety / efficacy studies</strong> (drug, crop, toxicology) &rarr; <strong>GLP</strong>, governed by the OECD Principles, not an accreditation like 17025.</li>
  <li><strong>Internal manufacturing lab</strong> &rarr; 17025 if your results leave the company, otherwise 9001 often suffices.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">Why ISO/IEC 17025 is the default</h2>
<p>Unlike ISO 9001, 17025 was written specifically for labs: it adds technical requirements for method validation, measurement uncertainty, equipment calibration and traceability. That is exactly what your customers&rsquo; auditors and regulators look for. The current edition is <strong>ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong> (third edition).</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">Accreditation bodies all assess the same 17025</h2>
<p>"Accreditation" is what a national body grants against 17025. The standard is identical; the body and its region differ:</p>
