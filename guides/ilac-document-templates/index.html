ILAC and the standard

ILAC (International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation) is not an accreditation body — it is the global Mutual Recognition Arrangement (MRA) that makes your accreditation portable across borders. Your national body (CNAS, A2LA, UKAS, NVLAP, NATA, SINGLAS, NABL and others) assesses you against ISO/IEC 17025:2017; ILAC MRA signatory status is what lets customers and regulators in other countries accept your reports.

For laboratories, the ILAC documents that matter most are the P-series policies: ILAC P8 (use of the ILAC MRA mark and combined marks on reports), ILAC R7 (joint use of accreditation symbols with other bodies), ILAC P9 (proficiency testing policy and frequencies), and ILAC P10 (metrological traceability policy). Your accreditation body enforces these through its own criteria, so your management system must show how reports, marks and PT results are controlled.

The core document set

  • Quality Manual — policy, scope, and the documented structure of your ISO/IEC 17025 system.
  • Report & Mark Control Procedure — how the ILAC MRA symbol and your body's mark are applied, and when they must be withheld (suspension, out-of-scope work, endorsed vs non-endorsed results).
  • Proficiency Testing Plan — participation mapped to ILAC P9 frequencies and your body's PT policy.
  • Traceability Procedure — metrological traceability statements consistent with ILAC P10.
  • Uncertainty of Measurement — a GUM or Monte Carlo budget per measurand, supporting decision-rule statements on reports.
