<h1 style="font-size:27px;margin:6px 0 6px;">ISO 17025:2025 doesn't exist — here's the truth</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;font-size:16px;margin-top:0;"><em>And why that matters if someone is selling you a "transition" right now.</em></p>

<p>If you work in a testing or calibration laboratory, you've probably seen the posts. A "complete guide to the new ISO/IEC 17025:2025." A webinar about preparing for the "2025 edition." A consultancy offering a migration package — deadline looming, act now.</p>

<p>Here's the uncomfortable truth, and it takes thirty seconds to verify: <strong>there is no ISO/IEC 17025:2025. Go to iso.org, find standard 66912, and look at the status line.</strong></p>
<p>(And if you want the opposite case — a <em>very</em> real change in the accreditation world that most content still hasn't caught up with — read our explainer on <a href="https://labkitpro.com/blog/global-aci-explained/" style="color:#2563eb;">Global ACI, the organisation that replaced IAF and ILAC on 1 January 2026</a>.)</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">What the official source actually says</h2>
<p>ISO publishes the status of every standard on its own catalogue. For ISO/IEC 17025, the page states:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Current edition: ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong> (Edition 3), published November 2017 (with a corrected version in March 2018)</li>
<li><strong>Stage: 90.93 — "International Standard confirmed"</strong> following its systematic review, which closed in 2023</li>
<li>In other words: the world's standards body formally reviewed 17025 and <strong>decided it remains current</strong>. No new edition has been published. No transition period is running. No "2025 requirements" exist to comply with.</li>
</ul>
<p>That's the whole fact-check. One webpage, thirty seconds.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">So where does the "2025 edition" rumor come from?</h2>
<p>Two places, and understanding them is genuinely useful — because this pattern will happen again with other standards.</p>
<p><strong>1. AI-generated content farms.</strong> Search for "ISO 17025:2025" and you'll find polished, confident, <em>detailed</em> articles — complete with a specific publication date, a list of "key changes" (digital calibration certificates! sustainability! software validation!), and a three-year transition timeline. We checked several of these while researching this post. Every one of them contradicts the official ISO catalogue. They read beautifully. They are fiction. Large language models generate plausible-sounding standard updates all the time, and low-effort sites publish them without checking. When several such sites agree with each other, that's not corroboration — they're copying the same mistake.</p>
<p><strong>2. Fear-based marketing.</strong> "The standard changed, you must upgrade" has always been the most reliable sales pitch in the compliance world. A fake new edition is even better for that pitch than a real one, because nothing constrains the details. If someone offers to sell you a course, a migration audit, or a documentation package for "ISO 17025:2025" — ask them one question: <em>which clause numbers changed?</em> A real transition has an official document behind it. A fake one has urgency.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">Hasn't a new version been "due" for a while?</h2>
<p>It's reasonable to expect a revision eventually — ISO standards go through systematic review every five years, and 17025's review concluded in 2023 with the decision to confirm the current edition. A future project may well update the standard again one day; when it does, it will move through ISO's public stages (proposal, committee draft, enquiry, approval) on the same catalogue page, and every accreditation body will talk about it loudly.</p>
<p>None of that has happened for 17025. Until you can see a new edition listed for sale on ISO's own site, any "next edition" is speculation — and any consultancy claiming its specifics is making them up.</p>
<p>(One nuance worth knowing: national adoptions like CNAS-CL01 in China or EN versions in Europe track the international standard. They can carry their own document numbers and implementation dates. None of them creates an "ISO 17025:2025" — there is no such ISO publication to adopt.)</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">What labs should actually do right now</h2>
<p>The boring, reassuring answer: <strong>keep strengthening your compliance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong> — the edition your certificate is issued against today. Concretely:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Treat the 2017 gap list as the work.</strong> Most labs' findings cluster in the same clauses: measurement traceability and uncertainty, document control, competence records, decision rules. Fixing those is never wasted — they'll be in any future revision too.</li>
<li><strong>Get your rumor filter right.</strong> The only sources worth trusting for edition status: the ISO catalogue page, your accreditation body's announcements, and your national standards body. Nothing else.</li>
<li><strong>Budget for audits, not for panic.</strong> No credible accreditation body has announced a 17025 transition deadline, because there is nothing to transition to.</li>
<li><strong>When a real revision comes, it will be loud.</strong> Accreditation bodies run multi-year transition programs with published policies — you will not miss it by learning about it from a blog post.</li>
</ol>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">The meta-lesson for lab people</h2>
<p>This episode is a small case study in something ISO 17025 itself trains us to do: <strong>verify against the source</strong>. You wouldn't accept a calibration result because "three websites said so" — you check the certificate and its traceability chain. Standards status deserves the same discipline. The next fake edition (of this or another standard) is already being drafted somewhere by a content farm. The thirty-second habit of checking the official catalogue is the vaccine.</p>

<h1 style="font-size:27px;margin:6px 0 6px;">ISO 17025:2025 并不存在——这才是真相</h1>
<p style="color:#5b6675;font-size:16px;margin-top:0;"><em>以及，为什么这件事与你身边正在兜售「过渡换版」的人有关。</em></p>

<p>如果你在检测或校准实验室工作，大概已经刷到过这些内容：一套「新版 ISO/IEC 17025:2025 完全指南」、一场关于「2025 版」准备工作的直播课，或者某咨询机构推出的「迁移服务包」——截止日期临近，请立即行动。</p>

<p>这里有一个令人不太舒服、但验证只需三十秒的真相：<strong>ISO/IEC 17025:2025 并不存在。</strong>打开 iso.org，找到标准 66912，看一眼它的状态栏。</p>
<p>（如果你想看反过来的案例——认可体系里一个<strong>千真万确</strong>、但多数内容还没跟上的大变化——请读我们的解读：<a href="https://labkitpro.com/blog/global-aci-explained/" style="color:#2563eb;">Global ACI：2026 年 1 月 1 日起取代 IAF 和 ILAC 的新组织</a>。）</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">官方来源实际说了什么</h2>
<p>ISO 会在自家目录里公布每项标准的实时状态。对 ISO/IEC 17025，页面上写得很清楚：</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>现行版本：ISO/IEC 17025:2017</strong>（第 3 版），2017 年 11 月发布（2018 年 3 月曾发布勘误版）</li>
  <li><strong>阶段：90.93——「国际标准已确认」</strong>，其系统评审已于 2023 年结束</li>
  <li>换句话说：全球标准机构正式复审了 17025，并<strong>决定维持现行版本</strong>。没有发布新版，没有过渡期，也不存在需要你遵守的「2025 要求」。</li>
</ul>
<p>这就是全部的事实核查。一个网页，三十秒。</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">那「2025 版」的谣言从何而来？</h2>
<p>来源有两个。搞清楚它们对你确实有用——因为同样的套路，以后还会出现在别的标准上。</p>
<h3 style="font-size:16px;margin-top:18px;color:#14202e;">1. AI 生成的内容农场</h3>
<p>搜索「ISO 17025:2025」，你会看到大量措辞漂亮、语气笃定、细节丰满的文章——有具体的发布日期、一长串「关键变化」（数字校准证书！可持续性！软件验证！）和一份三年过渡时间表。我们撰写本文时抽查了好几篇，每一篇都与 ISO 官方目录相互矛盾。它们读起来行云流水，但全是虚构。大语言模型随时能生成听起来煞有介事的标准更新，而低成本的网站不做任何核实就照单发布。当好几家这样的网站口径一致时，那不叫互相印证——它们只是在复制同一个错误。</p>
<h3 style="font-size:16px;margin-top:18px;color:#14202e;">2. 贩卖焦虑的营销</h3>
<p>「标准变了，你必须升级」向来是合规圈最灵验的销售话术。而一个虚构的新版比真版更好用，因为没有任何细节能约束它。如果有人向你推销「ISO 17025:2025」的课程、迁移审核或文档包，就问他们一个问题：<strong>到底改了哪些条款号？</strong>真正的换版背后有一份官方文件撑腰，而假货只有紧迫感。</p>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">新版不是「早就该出了」吗？</h2>
<p>期待未来某一天修订，是合理的：ISO 标准每五年经历一次系统评审，17025 的评审在 2023 年收尾，结论是维持现行版本。将来某个项目确实可能再次更新这项标准；真到那时，它会沿着 ISO 的公开流程推进（提案、委员会草案、询问、批准），在同一个目录页面上全程可见，所有认可机构也都会大张旗鼓地讨论。</p>
<p>但这些事目前一件都没有发生在 17025 身上。在你看到 ISO 官网挂出新版待售之前，任何「下一版」都只是猜测——而任何声称掌握其具体内容的咨询机构，都是在编故事。</p>
<div style="border:1px solid #dde9f0;border-left:4px solid #4a7bb5;background:#eef4fa;border-radius:10px;padding:15px 18px;margin:18px 0;font-size:14.5px;"><b>一个值得知道的细节：</b>中国 CNAS-CL01、欧洲 EN 版这类国家采纳版本会跟随国际标准，它们有自己的文件编号和实施日期。但没有任何一份此类文件能创造「ISO 17025:2025」——因为根本不存在这样一份可供采纳的 ISO 出版物。</div>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">实验室现在真正该做什么</h2>
<p>答案有些平淡，但令人安心：<strong>继续深化你对 ISO/IEC 17025:2017 的符合性</strong>——也就是你当前证书所依据的那个版本。具体来说：</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>把 2017 版的不符合项清单当作工作主线。</strong>多数实验室的问题集中在同样的条款上：测量溯源与不确定度、文件控制、能力记录、判定规则。修正这些永远不会白费——未来任何修订版里，它们依然在。</li>
  <li><strong>装好你的谣言过滤器。</strong>在版本状态这件事上，值得信任的来源只有三个：ISO 目录页面、你的认可机构公告、你的国家标准机构。其余一概不算数。</li>
  <li><strong>预算花在审核上，别花在恐慌上。</strong>没有任何一家有信誉的认可机构宣布过 17025 的换版截止日期，因为根本没有可换的版本。</li>
  <li><strong>真正的修订来临时，动静会很大。</strong>认可机构会推出长达数年的过渡项目并发布公开政策——你绝不会因为「只从一篇博客文章得知」而错过它。</li>
</ol>

<h2 style="font-size:19px;margin-top:24px;">给实验室人的元教训</h2>
<p>这件事，是 ISO 17025 本身训练我们的那套方法的小型案例：<strong>对照源头核实。</strong>你不会因为「三个网站都这么说」就接受一个校准结果——你会去查证书和它的溯源链。标准的状态，值得享受同样的严谨。下一个虚构新版（无论是这条还是别的标准）此刻大概已经在某个内容农场里起草了。而那个「三十秒查官方目录」的习惯，就是疫苗。</p>
