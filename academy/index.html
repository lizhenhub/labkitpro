0. Four-Tier Document System Overview

Management system documents are organized in four tiers following the standard pyramid model: Level A (Quality Manual) is the top-level policy document; Level B (Procedures) describes cross-functional processes; Level C (Work Instructions) provides position-level operational detail; Level D (Forms & Records) carries the evidence of execution. Tiers must cross-reference each other.

A · Quality ManualPolicy, objectives, element statements, master file index
▼ references & expands
B · Procedures (LP)Cross-functional workflows: responsibilities, steps, records (e.g. equipment management, contract review)
▼ detailed to position level
C · Work Instructions / SOPPosition-level operations: step-by-step instructions, parameters, acceptance criteria (e.g. balance calibration)
▼ produces evidence
D · Forms & Records (FR)Execution evidence: raw records, ledgers, reports, approval forms

Role and writing granularity of the four tiers

TierQuestion it answersTypical chapter structureAudit focus
A Quality ManualWhat does the lab commit to? (policy / objectives / system scope)Cover · Authorization · Amendment record · Scope · Policy & objectives · Element statements · File listCovers all applicable clauses; policy and objectives actually deployed
B ProceduresHow is it done? (process, responsibilities, control points)Purpose · Scope · Responsibilities · Procedure · Records · ReferencesSteps are closed-loop; responsibilities clear; records match
C Work InstructionsExactly how, step by step? (position-level, followable)Purpose · Scope · Prerequisites · Equipment · Steps · Calculations · RecordsReproducible detail; quantified parameters; a new staff member can follow it
D Forms & RecordsWhat was done? (evidence)Header (number/version) · data fields · signature & date · archivingTraceable; amendments traceable; retention compliant
General writing principles: use one unified standard structure across all tiers; write short simple sentences, common words and active voice — never write essays; explain technical terms in footnotes or inline; documents must match actual practice (verify the current reality before writing); describe each element as a logical segment: Policy → Objectives → Responsible role → Referenced procedure → Related records.
1. Level A · Quality Manual (QM) Chapter Structure

The Quality Manual is the top-level system document. ISO/IEC 17025:2017 does not mandate a fixed format, but the manual must accurately convey the quality policy, objectives, references to lower-tier documents and system management responsibilities. The recommended chapter sequence below follows the standard's clause order for element descriptions.

Amendment recordSequence, amendment no., page, clause, date, content of change, reason, QA team and supervisor signatures8.3.2 c)

1.3 Four-part element description template (directly reusable)

PolicyThis laboratory commits to … (one-sentence statement consistent with the corresponding ISO/IEC 17025 clause)
ObjectivesMeasurable objective(s) for this element (e.g. equipment calibration timeliness ≥ 98%)
ResponsibilityRole/function responsible for executing or supervising this element (e.g. technical manager, equipment coordinator)
Referenced proceduresProcedure "…" No. LP-XXX (detail lives in the Level B document; overview only here)
Related recordsFR-XXX "… record" and others (evidence is produced here)
Example (4.1 Impartiality): Policy — The laboratory is responsible for the impartiality of its activities and allows no commercial, financial or other pressures to compromise it. Objective — 100% coverage of the annual impartiality risk assessment. Responsibility — Quality manager, impartiality committee. Procedure — LP-001 Impartiality & Risk Management Procedure. Records — FR-001 Impartiality risk identification & evaluation form, signed codes of conduct.
2. Level B · Procedures (LP) Chapter Structure

Procedures describe cross-functional flows — who, when, where, what and how. Based on widely used technical writing conventions for management system procedures, a recommended unified eight-section skeleton plus supporting sections:

2.1 Unified procedure chapter skeleton

ChapterElements it must containWriting essentials
Document headerDocument No. (LP-XXX), version, effective date, prepared/reviewed/approved signaturesConsistent with manual/form numbering scheme
1 PurposeIntent of this procedure; the ISO/IEC 17025 clause it satisfiesOne sentence to one paragraph; may include conformity evaluation statement
2 ScopeApplicable departments and activity types; explicit exceptionsInclude precision/accuracy/applicability boundaries
3 ResponsibilitiesRoles and authorities within this processRoles, not names
4 Definitions & abbreviationsTerms and abbreviations specific to this processMay reference VIM / standard definitions
5 ProcedureStep-by-step flow (steps, decision points, control points, key parameters, abnormal handling); flowchart optionalCore chapter; must be closed-loop and auditable
6 RecordsList of record/form numbers generated by this procedure and retention periodsOne-to-one with Level D forms
7 ReferencesManual chapters, other procedures, work instructions, external standards referencedComplete cross-references
8 AppendicesFlowcharts, worksheets, templates, calculation notesSupporting material
A proven procedure structure used in technical documentation: 1 Introduction (1.1 Purpose / 1.2 Definitions / 1.3 Traceability chain definition / 1.5 Prerequisites / 1.6 Responsibility / 1.7 Safety) → 2 Methodology (2.1 Summary / 2.2 Procedure) → 3 Calculations → 4 Assignment of Uncertainty → References → Appendices. The high-value sections — Prerequisites, Responsibility and Safety — are folded into chapters 4 / 3 / 5 of the skeleton above.

4. Level D · Forms & Records (FR) Structure and List

Forms are the sole carrier of records and the most direct evidence auditors examine. Following recognized record-keeping practice, a form must satisfy: unique identification, complete fields, traceability, auditable amendments, and a defined retention period.

4.1 Unified form structure

Header zoneForm name, number (FR-XXX), version, effective date, page number (Page X of Y)
Information zoneObject identification (equipment no. / item no. / personnel name), date & time, location/conditions, referenced document number
Data zoneField names, units, reading/result columns, judgment column; mandatory fields explicitly marked
Control zoneRecorded-by signature & date, reviewed-by signature & date, remark area for exceptions
Archive zoneRetention period, archive location, confidentiality level
Record amendment rule (ISO/IEC 17025:2017 §7.5.2): original data must never be overwritten or erased — strike through to keep the original value, note the correction beside it, and record the amender's signature and date; electronic records must leave a system audit trail.

6. General Writing Rules & Ten-Step Process

6.1 Writing rules

  • Write short simple sentences with common words; use active voice, not passive.
  • Explain technical terms with footnotes or inline notes so readers of different levels understand.
  • Do not write essays — use itemized, process-style expression.
  • Documents must match current actual practice — verify the current reality first, then write.
  • Write for the document's users (executors / auditors); keep a unified structure for quick navigation.
  • Break each element into logical segments: Policy / Objectives / Responsibility / Procedure reference / Record reference (see 1.3).
  • Declare non-applicable clauses explicitly in the manual and state the reason.

6.2 Ten-step writing process for the manual / procedures

1 CollectCollect all documents currently in use in the lab and compile a list
2 ClassifySort documents into clause folders per ISO/IEC 17025 structure
3 MapList each process and its interrelations and application scenarios
4 VerifyAnalyze whether actual work follows the documents
5 ReviseWhere documents disagree with practice, revise the document or improve the practice
6 DraftDraft the manual/procedures; check full coverage of standard requirements
7 CirculateRoute for review by all staff; collect comments
8 EditIncorporate comments; align with the standard
9 Read throughRead the full text to confirm clarity and unambiguity
10 ReleaseAuthorize release and use; later changes follow the controlled process

Free · Measurement uncertainty, in plain words

Free
  • Measurement uncertainty = the "confidence band" around your result; without it, testing/calibration results are not acceptable to any accreditation body.
  • The GUM method (analytical) fits simple, well-modelled quantities; Monte Carlo (MCM) fits non-linear cases with overlapping distributions.
  • Every report/certificate must state uncertainty — a hard §7.6 requirement and a frequent nonconformity at assessment.
