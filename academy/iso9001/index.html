What actually changed (ISO-confirmed)Seven ISO-confirmed changes in the new edition — nothing invented.▾

What actually changed (ISO-confirmed)

  • 5.1 Leadership — Strengthened: explicit quality culture and ethical behaviour; leaders must create an environment where everyone actively contributes to QMS effectiveness.
  • 5.2 Quality policy — More explicitly linked to context (4.1) and strategic direction — the QMS becomes a tool for business objectives, not just compliance.
  • 6.1 Risk & opportunity — Clearer separation of addressing risks vs pursuing opportunities — each used for informed decision-making and continual improvement, not blurred together.
  • 7.3 Awareness — New requirement: people must understand quality culture and ethical behaviour, not just know the procedures.
  • New Annex A — Replaces the old informative annex; clarifies key concepts, terminology and clause intent so the standard is easier to apply consistently.
  • Climate change — The 2024 amendment’s climate-change consideration is carried into the main text (4.1/4.2).
  • Harmonized structure — Adopts the latest harmonized structure for ISO management-system standards, making integration with 14001/45001 easier.

Gap checklist (tick off as you close)Six executable actions — tick each one off to close your gaps.▾

Gap checklist (tick off as you close)

  • Re-read 5.1: add explicit quality-culture and ethical-behaviour expectations and evidence into your leadership process/roles.
  • Check the quality policy (5.2) explicitly aligns with strategic direction; rewrite the intro if it does not.
  • Split your 6.1 risk register and opportunity register — are they currently blended into one?
  • Update 7.3 awareness material to include quality culture and ethical behaviour, and keep training evidence.
  • Verify 4.1/4.2 cover climate-change considerations where relevant.
  • Use the new Annex A as an internal training reference to align everyone’s understanding.

What the auditor will probe at the transition auditThe four questions most likely on transition-audit day — prepare your answers.▾

What the auditor will probe at the transition audit

  • Can leadership articulate what “quality culture” concretely means here and how it is measured?
  • Is there documented or minuted evidence that the quality policy aligns with strategic direction?
  • Are risks and opportunities managed separately, and are opportunities actually used in improvement decisions?
  • Do employees understand quality culture and ethical behaviour, not just recite the policy?
