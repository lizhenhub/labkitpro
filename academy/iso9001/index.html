What actually changed (ISO-confirmed)Ten ISO- and BSI-confirmed changes, each with a “what it means on the ground” line — not just what changed, but what it forces you to do.▾
TP-9001-01Applies to：ISO 9001:2026 Transition PackRev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01What actually changed (ISO-confirmed)

  • 5.1 Leadership: quality culture & ethics — Explicit quality culture and ethical behaviour; leaders must create an environment where everyone actively contributes to QMS effectiveness. On the ground: culture can no longer be a slogan — define 3-5 observable behaviours, 2-3 measurable indicators (first-pass yield, rework / cost of quality, awareness surveys) and keep evidence of day-to-day leadership engagement.
  • 5.2 Policy: aligned with strategy — The policy is more explicitly linked to context (4.1) and strategic direction — the QMS becomes a tool for business objectives, not a compliance ornament. On the ground: rewrite the policy intro, cite the strategy explicitly, and show the “policy → objectives → performance” chain at management review.
  • 6.1 Risk & opportunity: formally split — A clear separation between addressing risks (for informed decision-making) and pursuing opportunities (for continual improvement). On the ground: keep two registers — risks and opportunities — and tie every opportunity to an improvement project with an owner and a due date.
  • 7.3 Awareness: from knowing to understanding — New: people must understand quality culture and ethical behaviour, not just know the procedures. On the ground: upgrade training from process briefings to case-based culture modules, and keep sign-in sheets, quiz results and communication records.
  • Change management made explicit — The 2026 edition states change-management requirements more explicitly (a highlighted item in BSI’s clause-by-clause comparison): changes need planning, review and controlled implementation. On the ground: consolidate scattered operational change controls into one clear flow — trigger, impact assessment, approval, verification, closure — with evidence at each step.
  • New Annex A (15 pages of guidance) — It replaces the old informative annex (Annex B is removed) with 15 pages clarifying concepts, terminology and clause intent. On the ground: it is the best single reference for internal training — translate each clarification into your organization’s own language and keep the training evidence.
  • Climate change in the main text — The Amd1:2024 climate-change consideration is carried into the main text (4.1/4.2). On the ground: your context analysis must show a conclusion on climate — analyse the impact if relevant, and record the assessment either way; leaving it blank is a nonconformity.
  • Terminology & structure harmonized — Terms and definitions are updated to match the latest harmonized structure (≈23 additions/updates), aligning more closely with 14001/45001. On the ground: run a site-wide wording review of controlled documents — especially leadership, culture and risk vocabulary — to prepare for integrated management.
  • Documented information: “retain” → “available” — Documented-information requirements shift toward an “availability” basis. On the ground: review your documented-information list — relax what can move from “must retain” to “available on demand”, and strengthen access to what truly matters (culture indicators, opportunity-loop status).
  • Certification calendar — Published 2026-09-16; from 2028-03-31 new initial certificates are issued only to the 2026 edition; 2029-09-30 is the transition cutoff when 2015 certificates lapse. On the ground: your surveillance/recertification window IS your transition window — schedule the transition audit into your next surveillance visit to save cost.

01Clause-by-clause reading

Clause 3Terms and definitions

Terminology around quality culture and ethical behaviour enters the leadership-responsibility context, with definitions harmonized across the structure. Push the wording changes into controlled documents rather than leaving them at interpretation level.

Clause 4Context of the organization

Climate change and sustainability are now explicit in the context. Your QMS must reflect these priorities: meet interested-party expectations, demonstrate ESG alignment and stay competitive in a sustainability-driven market.

Clause 5Leadership

Ethical behaviour and quality culture are part of leadership responsibility. Top management should show visible commitment — new training, open communication and leading by example — so the values are practised across the organization.

Clause 6Planning

Risks and opportunities are formally separated, with stronger “opportunity-based thinking”. Organizations need a clearer resilience-planning structure showing how risk response and proactive opportunity-seeking balance.

Clause 7Support

Awareness now includes quality culture and ethical behaviour. Training, communication and development must go beyond the basics — people at every level should live the values daily.

Clause 8Operation (low impact)

Operation sees only minor adjustments, mainly terminology. Ensure updated clause references stay consistent and traceable; overall operational impact is small — a low-risk zone of your transition project.

Clause 9Performance evaluation (unchanged core)

Core performance-evaluation requirements are unchanged: monitoring, measurement, audits and management review continue as before. The focus is not new processes but better use of data — demonstrating improvement without added complexity.

Clause 10Improvement

Leadership’s role in driving continual improvement is now more explicit. Provide evidence that leadership actively influences the improvement cycle and supports it — beyond merely applying corrective actions.

Annex AStructure, terminology and clause clarification (15+ pages)

For the first time, ISO 9001 provides supplementary guidance via an annex: 15+ pages of substantive clarification on structure, terminology and clauses. A key benefit for new and existing users alike — use it as the shared baseline for training and internal audits.

TP-9001-04Applies to：ISO 9001:2026 Transition PackRev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01Gap checklist (tick off as you close)

  • Give “quality culture” an operational definition: pick 3-5 observable behaviours (e.g. stop-the-line authority, nonconformity reporting rate) and 2-3 indicators (first-pass yield, rework / cost of quality, awareness survey), with measurement frequency and owners. Done when: the definition is in a controlled document, indicators are on a dashboard, and someone owns the data.
  • Rewrite the quality-policy intro: cite strategic direction and 4.1 context explicitly. Done when: the new policy is published with briefing and sign-off records.
  • Split 6.1 into two registers: risks and opportunities managed separately. Done when: every opportunity links to an improvement project with owner and due date, and at least one has closed the loop.
  • Upgrade 7.3 awareness material: add case-based modules on quality culture and ethics. Done when: everyone is trained, with sign-ins, quizzes and communication records filed.
  • Add the climate analysis to 4.1/4.2: analyse impacts on interested parties and scope if relevant; record the assessment either way. Done when: the context document is updated and signed off.
  • Run internal training against Annex A: translate the 15 pages of clarifications into your own language. Done when: training is complete with materials and sign-in evidence.
  • Site-wide terminology review: reconcile controlled documents against the 2026 terms (≈23 changes). Done when: a term-mapping table exists and affected documents are revised and re-issued.
  • Review the documented-information list on the “availability” basis. Done when: the list is updated with recorded rationale for each relax/strengthen decision.
  • Consolidate change management: trigger, impact assessment, approval, verification, closure — evidence at every step. Done when: the process is published and at least one real change in the last 6 months shows a full trail.
  • Align internal audit and management review: re-cut checklists to the new clauses; add strategy alignment, culture and opportunity usage as standing management-review items. Done when: the new checklist has run once and review outputs include decisions on all three new items.

What the auditor will probe at the transition auditThe eight questions most likely on transition-audit day, with an answer-card method — for each, the form of evidence the auditor is really listening for.▾
TP-9001-06Applies to：ISO 9001:2026 Transition PackRev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01What the auditor will probe at the transition audit

  • Can leadership articulate what “quality culture” concretely means here and how it is measured? — the auditor wants behaviours and indicators, not values posters.
  • Is there documented or minuted evidence the policy aligns with strategic direction? — show the policy→objectives→performance passage in your review minutes.
  • Are risks and opportunities managed separately, and did opportunities actually drive improvement? — tell one full story from register to closed improvement.
  • Do employees understand quality culture and ethics, not just recite the policy? — floor interviews: ask what people do first when they hit a quality problem.
  • Has one change in the last 6 months gone through the new flow? Show the full trail. — trigger, impact, approval, verification, closure; a missing step gets flagged.
  • How was climate change handled in 4.1/4.2? — even if not applicable, show the assessment record; “we never looked at it” is a nonconformity.
  • Did Annex A’s clarifications land in training? — ask any internal auditor or team lead to define a concept from Annex A.
  • Is ethical behaviour anchored in a mechanism? — check that the nonconformity channel is anonymously usable and that anti-retaliation handling records exist.

02Answer-card preparation (one page per probe)

  • Write one answer card per probe: a one-line conclusion + evidence index (document/record numbers) + owner + data source.
  • Bind evidence by clause number into a “transition evidence pack” — anything findable within 30 seconds on audit day.
  • Rehearse one interviewee per level (top / middle / floor), consistent but not scripted — auditors spot rehearsals instantly.
  • For unfinished items, bring a remediation plan (milestones + owners); honesty beats concealment — transition audits allow plans, not hiding.
  • Walk the inspection route in advance: shop floor, records room, training room — 2-3 staged “show this” points per stop.
  • Carry the gap-analysis report — it is the first piece of evidence an auditor uses to judge your transition maturity.

Evidence-prep checklist for transition-audit dayEight evidence classes, item by item: every record the auditor will ask for on transition-audit day — archived early, clause-indexed, retrievable in 30 seconds.▾
TP-9001-08Applies to：ISO 9001:2026 Transition PackRev 2026.1 · prepared 2026-10

01Evidence-prep checklist for transition-audit day

  • Gap-analysis report: clause-by-clause 2015→2026, with gap descriptions, ownership matrix, closure plan and a sign-off page.
  • Revision history of revised controlled documents: revision pages, approval records, issue/withdrawal logs, void stamps.
  • Culture-indicator dashboard export: trend charts for first-pass yield, rework / cost of quality and awareness surveys, with management commentary.
  • Opportunity-to-improvement case file: the full chain from a register entry to project initiation, verification and closure.
  • Training & awareness evidence: Annex A training materials, sign-in sheets, quizzes, communication artifacts and coverage statistics.
  • Policy-launch evidence: the new policy, briefing records, sign-off sheets and minutes excerpts showing strategic alignment.
  • Change-management case: at least one real change with all five steps documented (trigger → impact → approval → verification → closure).
  • Audit & review outputs: internal-audit report against the new clauses, closed nonconformities, and management-review decisions covering the three new items.
