ISO 9001 Quality Manual — What Changed in 2015, What to Include, How to Write One

ISO 9001:2015 removed the standalone quality-manual requirement that existed in the 2008 edition. What replaced it matters more: Clause 4.4 requires every QMS process to be documented, and Clause 7.5 governs all documented information. This page shows what a modern manual must contain and how to generate one that satisfies both the standard and your auditor.

Did ISO 9001:2015 remove the quality manual?

Yes and no. The 2008 edition’s Clause 4.2.1 mandatory requirement for a quality manual is gone. But the 2015 edition did not remove the obligation to document your quality management system — it redistributed it. Clause 4.4.2 requires the organization to maintain documented information to the extent necessary to support the operation of its processes, and Clause 7.5 sets the rules for creating, updating and controlling that information. So a quality manual is now optional in name, but the documented information it used to hold is still mandatory.

What a 2015-era manual should contain

A useful manual still does the jobs auditors expect: it states the scope of the QMS (Clause 4.3), shows the sequence and interaction of processes (Clause 4.4), and references the documented procedures and work instructions the organization actually uses. Because the standard is built on Annex SL, the manual should map directly onto Clauses 4–10. Keep it lean — its job is to be the map, not the territory.

Where manuals fail audits

The most common findings are not missing manuals but manuals that don’t match reality: a process described on paper that no one follows, a scope that excludes activities the company actually performs, and uncontrolled copies circulating because Clause 7.5.3 (control of documented information) isn’t enforced. A manual is only as good as the system it describes.

Frequently asked questions

Is a quality manual still required for ISO 9001 certification?

Not as a standalone document — Clause 4.2.1 was removed in 2015. However, you must retain enough documented information to demonstrate your QMS conforms to the standard. Most organizations keep a manual because it remains the clearest way to show that.

Can one manual cover ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001?

Yes. All three share the Annex SL high-level structure (Clauses 4–10), so an integrated manual with shared clauses 4–10 and standard-specific sections is the most efficient approach and is widely accepted by certification bodies.

What is the difference between a quality manual and a procedure?

The manual is the top-level overview — scope, policy, process map. Procedures explain how a specific process is performed. You need both: the manual shows the system; the procedures run it.
